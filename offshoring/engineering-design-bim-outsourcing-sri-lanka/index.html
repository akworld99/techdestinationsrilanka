Engineering design, BIM and quantity surveying outsourcing to Sri Lanka

Sri Lankan engineering and design firms deliver BIM modelling, Revit and CAD drafting, structural detailing, 3D visualisation and quantity surveying for construction clients in the UK, Australia and the Gulf. Quantity surveying is a well established profession in Sri Lanka with strong links to Gulf construction, and university research identifies BIM modelling and bill of quantities preparation as well suited to outsourcing. This is a growing, specialist area with smaller firms rather than large centres.

AI: what is being automated, and what is not

Automated take off and AI assisted drafting are improving, but BIM coordination, clash resolution, code compliant detailing and quantity surveying judgement remain skilled work. Exposure is lower here than in most back office categories.

What is BIM and CAD outsourcing?

BIM and CAD outsourcing means using an external team to produce building information models, drawings, detailing, quantity take off and estimates for your projects, to your standards and templates.

How to outsource engineering, bim and quantity surveying to Sri Lanka, step by step

  1. Pick one process

    Choose a single, well documented process with clear volumes and quality measures, such as accounts payable or tier two support.

  2. Decide the model

    A managed provider runs it for you; a dedicated team works only for you through a staffing provider or employer of record; your own centre comes later.

  3. Shortlist two or three providers

    Ask each the questions below, check references in your country and meet the people who would do the work.

  4. Run a paid pilot

    Four to eight weeks with clear targets, with your team reviewing output daily at first.

  5. Agree the contract

    Service levels, data protection, IP and confidentiality, pricing, notice and exit terms.

  6. Scale what works

    Add people or processes once quality is proven, and keep a named lead on both sides.

Questions to ask a Sri Lankan provider

  • Which standards have they worked to, such as UK BIM, RIBA stages or NRM?
  • Which software versions and templates can they use?
  • How are model quality and clash checks reviewed?
  • Can they show work on similar building types?
  • How is your project data stored and shared?
