Why Sri Lanka

Eight reasons, each backed by a company you can look up. Every claim on this page links to a story with its sources.

  1. The work is core product, and has been for decades

    IFS does about 60% of its global product development in Colombo (our analysis). Sysco's engineers here work on the same codebase as its teams in Texas. Synopsys engineers chip design software here, and Zebra Technologies builds product for its global platforms.

  2. Sri Lankan founders build companies that get bought

    The London Stock Exchange bought the Colombo company that built its trading platform. EQT acquired WSO2 for $600 million. Virtusa listed on Nasdaq and sold for $2 billion. JPMorganChase acquired a Sri Lankan founded wealth platform in 2026.

  3. European and Australian companies stay for the long run

    Colombo teams have worked with Scandinavian clients for 25 years. A Stockholm listed software group calls Colombo its global tech center. Dutch and Australian companies have built their engineering base here and kept growing it.

  4. The talent pipeline is measured

    12,000+ ICT graduates a year and third in Asia for affordable tech talent, according to Startup Genome 2026. 120,000+ IT professionals and $1.645 billion in exports (export data). Moratuwa and SLIIT feed it.

  5. It is more than software engineers

    Sri Lanka's depth in certified accountants supports finance operations for clients in 10+ countries, and US healthcare revenue operations run from Colombo under HITRUST certification.

  6. The working day overlaps with yours

    GMT+5:30 gives four to six hours of real time overlap with Europe and three to four with the US East Coast, and a full day with the Middle East and much of Asia Pacific (analysis).

  7. The infrastructure is already built

    Purpose built technology campuses in Colombo house 85+ technology companies, and Port City Colombo's special economic zone has its first global tenants.

  8. Startups are building products, beyond services

    Sri Lankan startups ship their own AI products, and one builds AI infrastructure for the Estonian government. The country has about 750 to 1,000 startups. See the startup ecosystem.
