How to pay employees and contractors in Sri Lanka

A foreign company can pay people in Sri Lanka in three ways. Contractors invoice you and you pay them abroad, in dollars if you like, and they settle their own tax at a maximum of 15%. Employees are paid in rupees through local payroll, either by an employer of record or by your own Sri Lankan company, with 15% added for provident and trust fund contributions. You cannot run Sri Lankan payroll yourself without a local entity.

Last reviewed 23 October 2026. Rules changed in 2025 and 2026. This is general information, not legal or tax advice. US dollar figures use LKR 331 per dollar. How we check

There is a fourth option: a dedicated team employed by a Sri Lankan company that invoices you monthly. Payroll is their job. See dedicated development teams.

Paying contractors directly

This is the simplest route and the one most foreign startups use first. The process:

  1. Check it really is contracting

    Defined deliverables, their own hours and tools, freedom to work for other clients. If you need someone full time, inside your team and under your direction indefinitely, that is employment. Use an employer of record instead.

  2. Sign a services agreement

    Before any work starts, with the clauses listed below. Sign electronically and keep the signed copy.

  3. Agree the currency and schedule

    Most Sri Lankan contractors prefer US dollars, pounds or euros, monthly in arrears. Fix the rate in the contract so currency moves are not renegotiated every month.

  4. Get an invoice every period

    With their name, address, the period, the work done and bank details. It is your record of a business to business payment.

  5. Pay into their Sri Lankan bank account

    By SWIFT transfer, a money transfer service, or a contractor platform such as Deel or Remote. Paying into a Sri Lankan bank is what lets the contractor claim the 15% tax cap, so do not pay into an account abroad.

  6. Leave the tax to them

    The contractor declares the income in Sri Lanka. As a foreign payer with no Sri Lankan presence you do not normally withhold. Take advice if you have an office, a company or staff there.

How Sri Lankan contractors are taxed

  • From 1 April 2025, income from services provided abroad and paid in foreign currency is taxed at a maximum of 15% when remitted through a Sri Lankan bank (TaxAdvisor.lk).
  • The first LKR 150,000 a month (about USD 455) is tax free, as for every individual (Ada Derana).
  • Income left offshore is taxed at the normal progressive rates of up to 36%. This is why good contractors will ask you to pay into their Sri Lankan account.
  • Service export proceeds must reach Sri Lanka within 180 days of the service, and service exporters are not required to convert them into rupees (Daily FT, June 2026). Many contractors keep a foreign currency account for exactly this.

What the contract needs

The same list works for a contractor agreement or, with employment terms added, an offer letter through an employer of record.

  • Scope and deliverables. What they deliver, not the hours they sit at a desk.
  • Fees, currency and payment terms. Rate, currency, invoice cycle, days to pay, who bears bank charges.
  • Intellectual property assignment. Everything they create for you is assigned to your company on creation, with a waiver of moral rights where possible. Never rely on default rules.
  • Confidentiality. Covering your code, customers and roadmap, surviving the end of the contract.
  • Data protection. If they touch personal data, processing terms aligned with the Personal Data Protection Act, whose core obligations apply from 1 January 2027 (Daily FT).
  • Independence. They control how and when they work, use their own equipment and may take other clients. Say it and mean it.
  • Termination. Notice on both sides, often 30 days, and what happens to work in progress.
  • Governing law and disputes. Your home law with arbitration is common. Labour protections under Sri Lankan law can still apply if the relationship turns out to be employment.

For employees, Sri Lankan law also requires written particulars: designation, hours, pay and frequency, allowances, overtime, probation, leave and holidays (De Saram). Probation can run up to 180 days, extendable once.

Paying employees

Employees on Sri Lankan payroll are paid monthly, in rupees, into a local bank account, with a payslip showing every deduction (Playroll). Running that payroll needs a registered local employer, so a foreign company without an entity has two choices:

  • Employer of record. The provider employs the person, runs payroll and files everything. You pay one monthly invoice in your currency. See our employer of record guide for providers that cover Sri Lanka.
  • Your own company. Register a Sri Lankan company, which can be fully foreign owned, and run payroll yourself. See the company setup guide.

  • Gratuity. At employers with 15 or more workers, anyone who leaves after five years gets half a month's salary per completed year. For this engineer that is LKR 200,000 a year of service, so set aside about 4.2% of salary. An employer of record usually builds this into its fee or deposit.
  • Overtime. 1.5 times the hourly rate for staff covered by the Shop and Office Act. Executive grades are usually exempt.
  • Bonuses. Not required by law, but an annual bonus is common in the technology sector and expected in offers.

Running your own payroll: the monthly cycle

  1. Register once

    As an employer with the Employees' Provident Fund (run by the Central Bank), the Employees' Trust Fund Board and the Inland Revenue Department for withholding tax.

  2. Pay salaries monthly

    In rupees, by bank transfer, with a payslip.

  3. Pay withholding tax by the 15th

    APIT deducted in a month is due to the Inland Revenue Department by the 15th of the next month (MiHCM payroll guide).

  4. Pay EPF and ETF by month end

    Contributions for a month are due by the last working day of the following month.

  5. File annually

    Year end tax statements to employees, and keep a running gratuity provision.

Most new subsidiaries outsource this to a local payroll bureau or accounting firm for the first years.

Mistakes that cost money

  • Calling a full time team member a contractor. If they work only for you, on your hours, inside your team, a labour tribunal can treat them as an employee, with back contributions and termination protection to follow.
  • Paying into an account abroad. It costs your contractor the 15% cap and can push them to 36%, which comes back to you as a pay rise request.
  • No IP clause. Without a written assignment, ownership of code written by a contractor is open to argument.
  • Treating termination as at will. Ending employment after probation is regulated at employers with 15 or more staff (TEWA). Use probation properly and take advice before letting anyone go.
  • Ignoring the data law. From 1 January 2027 Sri Lanka's data protection obligations apply. Contracts signed now will still be running then.

Common questions

How do I pay a contractor in Sri Lanka?

Sign a services agreement, have the contractor invoice you, and pay by international bank transfer to their Sri Lankan bank account or through a contractor payment platform. You can pay in US dollars, pounds or euros. The contractor declares the income and pays their own Sri Lankan tax.

Can a foreign company pay Sri Lankan employees directly without a local entity?

Not as an employer in practice. Running Sri Lankan payroll means registering for the Employees' Provident Fund, the Employees' Trust Fund and income tax withholding, which needs a local employer. Without a company, use an employer of record or engage people as genuine contractors.

How are freelancers and contractors in Sri Lanka taxed?

From 1 April 2025, income an individual earns from services provided abroad and paid in foreign currency is taxed at a maximum of 15% if it is remitted through a Sri Lankan bank. The first LKR 150,000 a month of income is tax free. Income kept offshore is taxed at normal rates of up to 36%.

Do I have to withhold tax when I pay a Sri Lankan contractor?

A foreign company paying an independent contractor from abroad does not normally run Sri Lankan withholding. The contractor files their own return. Confirm with a tax adviser if you have any presence in Sri Lanka, because that changes the position.

What does it cost to employ someone in Sri Lanka on top of salary?

15% of gross earnings in statutory employer contributions: 12% to the Employees' Provident Fund and 3% to the Employees' Trust Fund. Employers with 15 or more workers also owe gratuity of half a month's salary per year of service to anyone who leaves after five years.

Can I pay Sri Lankan employees in US dollars?

Employees on local payroll are paid in Sri Lankan rupees into a local bank account. Contractors who export their services can be paid in foreign currency, and service exporters are not required to convert foreign currency proceeds into rupees.

What is the minimum wage in Sri Lanka in 2026?

LKR 30,000 a month, or LKR 1,200 a day, from 1 January 2026, under the National Minimum Wage of Workers Act as amended by Act No. 11 of 2025. Technology salaries are far above it.

When is a contractor treated as an employee in Sri Lanka?

When the relationship works like employment: you set the hours, they work only for you, they sit inside your team indefinitely and use your equipment. A labour tribunal looks at how the work is actually done, not the title on the contract. Misclassification can mean back payments of provident fund and trust fund contributions and termination claims.

Does Sri Lanka have a data protection law that affects employment contracts?

Yes. The Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022. Its core obligations on lawful processing, data protection officers, breach notification and cross border transfers apply from 1 January 2027. Contracts with people who handle your customer data should cover it now.
