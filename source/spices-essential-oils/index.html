Sri Lankan spice exporters: black pepper, cloves, nutmeg, essential oils and oleoresins

Sri Lanka exported US$449.9 million of spices, essential oils and oleoresins in 2025, and the sector grew 30.53% in the first nine months of 2026, with pepper up 61% to US$136.26 million. Beyond cinnamon, exporters supply black pepper, cloves, nutmeg and mace, cardamom, ginger and turmeric, essential oils from four main distilleries, 12 smaller distilleries and about 170 field units, and oleoresins. Pepper, cloves, nutmeg and cardamom are sold under the Ceylon Spices geographical indication. Most spices ship in bulk, unprocessed form.

Why buyers source spices and essential oils from Sri Lanka

  • Spices, essential oils and oleoresins earned US$449.91 million on 40,302 tonnes in 2025. EDB
  • Ceylon pepper, cloves, nutmeg and cardamom are branded under the Ceylon Spices geographical indication. EDB
  • Sri Lanka supplies about 6% of world pepper demand, and its pepper has a high piperine level. EDB
  • Sri Lanka is among the top ten clove exporters, producing about 5,301 tonnes a year. EDB
  • Essential oils come from four main distilleries, 12 small and medium distilleries and nearly 170 field distillation units. EDB
  • Pepper exports rose 61.45% to US$136.26 million in January to September 2026. EDB via Newswire

Certifications and standards to ask for

  • Certificate of origin, quarantine and quality certificates. The standard set for spice exports.
  • GMP, HACCP and ISO 22000. Ask which the processor holds.
  • Organic. Separate tariff lines exist for organic pepper, cloves and nutmeg; ask for the certificate and its scope.
  • Your market's residue limits. Agree pesticide residue and aflatoxin testing in the contract.

Know this before you buy

  • Earnings swing sharply: spices fell 2.59% in 2025, then rose 30.53% in January to September 2026. Expect price and availability to move. EDB via Newswire
  • Cardamom volumes are small, about 320 tonnes a year. EDB
  • Most spices ship in bulk, unprocessed form; confirm cleaning, sterilising and grinding capability if you need it. EDB

Questions to ask a Sri Lankan spice exporter

  • What density and moisture can you guarantee for pepper?
  • Can you clean, sort, sterilise and grind in house?
  • Which residue and aflatoxin tests do you run, and at which lab?
  • What volumes can you commit to each season?
  • Which organic and food safety certificates do you hold?

Common questions

Which spices can I source from Sri Lanka besides cinnamon?

Pepper, cloves, cardamom, nutmeg and mace, ginger, turmeric and vanilla, plus spice mixes and curry powder. Most are exported in bulk.

What makes Ceylon pepper different?

The Export Development Board says Sri Lankan pepper has a high piperine level. Sri Lanka meets about 6% of world pepper demand, and its main pepper markets are India, Germany and the US.

Which essential oils does Sri Lanka produce?

Cinnamon, pepper, cardamom, citronella, clove and nutmeg oils, mainly for perfumery, cosmetics and toiletries, plus spice oleoresins.

What documents come with a spice shipment?

A certificate of origin, a quarantine certificate and a quality certificate, with any food safety certificates the exporter holds.
