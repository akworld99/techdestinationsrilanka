Importing from Sri Lanka to Dubai and the UAE: what sells, duty and food rules

The UAE bought US$341 million of goods from Sri Lanka in 2025, and tea was the largest line, at US$101.5 million in bulk and packets. Coconut milk powder, desiccated coconut, cocoa preparations, clothing and king coconut follow. Imports pay the UAE's standard 5% customs duty, as no UAE and Sri Lanka trade agreement was in force in October 2026. In Dubai, food product approvals and import permits run through Dubai Municipality's digital systems.

Duty and trade access

  • The UAE charges 5% customs duty on the value of goods imported from abroad, with some goods at other rates. Sharjah Customs, customs tariff
  • No UAE and Sri Lanka Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement was in force in October 2026. In September 2026 Sri Lanka's Foreign Minister said Sri Lanka wanted it concluded faster. Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Sri Lanka
  • A separate investment protection agreement, not a trade deal, was signed in February 2025. Embassy of Sri Lanka, Abu Dhabi

Import rules to check in the UAE

  • Dubai Municipality's Montaji+ platform manages food product approvals and regulatory status for food imports and exports, with more than 1.7 million registered products. Dubai Municipality
  • Dubai's Unified Permit System handles food permit approvals and is linked to the emirate's food inspection checks. Dubai Municipality

Common questions

What is the import duty from Sri Lanka to the UAE?

The standard UAE customs duty is 5% of the value, with some goods at other rates. No UAE and Sri Lanka trade agreement was in force in October 2026, so there is no preference rate yet.

What does the UAE import from Sri Lanka?

Tea first: US$70.9 million in bulk and US$30.7 million in packets in 2025. Then petroleum oils, undergarments, cocoa preparations, coconut milk powder, women's outerwear, desiccated coconut, food preparations and king coconut.

Is there a UAE and Sri Lanka CEPA?

Not as of October 2026. Both sides have said they want one, and in September 2026 Sri Lanka's Foreign Minister asked for it to be concluded faster. A separate investment protection agreement was signed in February 2025.
